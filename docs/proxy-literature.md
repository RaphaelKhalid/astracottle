# Public working state as an oversight proxy: bounded literature and design review

Review date: 2026-09-08. This is a research synthesis and proposed follow-up design, not an experiment report or preregistration. The review used primary papers and official laboratory research pages. It is a bounded search, not an exhaustive novelty audit.

The defensible target is **an auditable information path that helps detect consequential mistakes**. Public state can be enforced as an input to a later call without being a transcript of that call's private computation. Three claims need different evidence: the state preserves useful information; the action depends on it; and an overseer benefits from seeing it. Success on one does not establish the others.

## Eight directly relevant sources

1. **Radhakrishnan et al. (2023), Question Decomposition Improves the Faithfulness of Model-Generated Reasoning.** The authors make models answer simpler subquestions in separate contexts and report improvements on several behavioral faithfulness measures, with a performance tradeoff. This is close prior art for enforced public intermediate outputs and context separation. It prevents claiming that separate-call decomposition itself is new. Their experiments do not establish how our controller behaves on Astra or whether a carried evidence record outperforms a separately generated reviewer aid. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11768).

2. **Lanham et al. (2023), Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning.** The authors perturb reasoning, including introducing mistakes and paraphrasing, and measure changes to answers. Reliance on the written reasoning varies substantially across tasks. This motivates content interventions and paraphrase controls, while reminding us that dependence is a behavioral measurement rather than full introspective access. Our Experiment 002 is adjacent to this intervention tradition; it should be described as an application-level public-input test. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13702).

3. **Lyu et al. (2023), Faithful Chain-of-Thought Reasoning.** Their framework translates a question into a symbolic representation with a language model and obtains the answer using a deterministic solver. The representation has an enforced role in the executed answer. The relevant design lesson is that stronger guarantees come from making a representation executable and checking its translation, rather than requesting more persuasive prose. For structured permission and release fixtures, a deterministic source-checked controller is a necessary practical comparison. Translation correctness remains a separate obligation. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13379).

4. **Jia, Benton and Easley (2026), Faithfulness as Information Flow.** This recent preprint separates sufficiency, completeness and necessity in a prompt-to-reasoning-to-answer pathway and tests training-time interventions. It is particularly close conceptual prior art for the bottleneck framing. Its attention-mask and gradient procedures require access our external controller does not have; replacing application inputs is a different intervention. We can measure preservation and behavioral dependence of public state, but should not import the paper's internal-information-flow claims into an API-only study. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24286), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24286v1).

5. **Chen et al. / Anthropic (2025), Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think.** On hint interventions, the tested models often used an answer hint without acknowledging it in their reasoning. In that study, longer explanations did not automatically mean more faithful explanations. This is evidence against treating an attractive or verbose public record as proof of how an answer arose. It supports auditing omitted or misstated facts independently rather than scoring the record's reassuring tone. The reported findings concern the tested models and interventions, not all future models. [Official research summary and paper link](https://www.anthropic.com/research/reasoning-models-dont-say-think).

6. **Emmons et al. (2025), When Chain of Thought is Necessary, Language Models Struggle to Evade Monitors.** The paper distinguishes runtime monitorability from complete faithfulness and investigates settings where executing the undesired behavior requires intermediate computation. It reports stronger monitoring in those settings, while retaining evasion limitations. That motivates decisions with real dependencies instead of gratuitous calculations. An externally forced evidence record is still not equivalent to making a particular model use readable token-level reasoning inside a call; necessity of supplying facts and necessity of verbal computation must remain distinct. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05246).

7. **OpenAI (2025), Evaluating Chain-of-Thought Monitorability / Monitoring Monitorability.** The official report defines monitorability through a monitor's ability to infer properties of behavior and separates intervention, process and outcome-property evaluations. It reports benefits from actual CoT in its evaluated settings, while noting that benign proxy evaluations need not predict real-world failures. It also studies follow-up questioning; its gains involve access to resulting CoT and cannot simply be assumed for public summaries. This supports reporting our outcome-detection results separately from record-dependence diagnostics. [Official research report](https://openai.com/index/evaluating-chain-of-thought-monitorability/).

8. **Lightman et al. (2023), Let's Verify Step by Step.** The authors compare training reward models using intermediate-step feedback and final-outcome feedback on mathematical problems; process supervision performs better in their evaluation. This supports the potential value of checkable intermediate steps, but is training evidence in a specific domain. A prompted review of a public record is not the same intervention, and the finding does not establish truthful introspection or oversight effectiveness on agentic policy decisions. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050).

## What remains worth testing

The useful narrow question is: **Does the exact evidence artifact supplied to an actor provide more oversight value than an equally resourced artifact independently prepared for the reviewer?** This distinguishes two practical explanations for improvement:

- **Presentation assistance:** either artifact helps the reviewer navigate difficult evidence.
- **Connection to the actor's input:** the carried artifact exposes an omission, mistaken interpretation or action inconsistency specific to the candidate being reviewed.

These explanations can coexist. A carried record might instead reduce detection by repeating the actor's mistaken interpretation and anchoring the monitor. Comparing against no record alone cannot distinguish these possibilities. A difference against an independently produced artifact still combines factual content, extraction quality and its connection to the actor; it is evidence about this intervention, not an identified effect of private reasoning or a pure causal effect of provenance.

The bounded search found substantial prior work on decomposition, causal interventions, executable intermediate representations and information-flow constraints. It did not establish that this exact operational, fixed-candidate comparison has already been evaluated. The appropriate contribution claim is a reproducible black-box mechanism study and benchmark, not an assertion of first invention. Even a null or adverse result can show that enforcing public state supplies no special oversight advantage beyond ordinary reviewer assistance in the evaluated setting.

## Proposed Experiment 005: fixed-candidate reviewer diagnostic

This design can follow the evidence-before-request experiment. It is a proposal until the case inventory, calls, prompts and analysis are frozen. It does not require new actor rollouts.

1. **Freeze the candidate population.** Include every eligible record-only candidate from one specified completed development cohort; specify eligibility using transport/schema rules, never correctness or favorable monitor effects. Publish all exclusions and attempted calls. Keep inaccurate carried records unchanged. Retain the original tasks, source evidence, requests, records and actions as immutable inputs. If several requests share a world or record, preserve that clustering in analysis.

2. **Generate an independent artifact per original evidence-commitment unit.** Use a fresh call with the exact pre-request evidence, policy, extraction instructions, schema and maximum size used for the carried record. Do not supply the eventual request, candidate action, carried record, grader labels or earlier monitor judgments. Generate it once; do not select the best of several outputs. Matching the generation budget is feasible; actual output lengths may differ and should be reported. Exact length matching must not use selective regeneration or fact truncation.

3. **Run three independent reviews of each saved candidate.** Every review sees identical original evidence, policy, request and action. The views add (a) no artifact, (b) the exact carried artifact, or (c) the independently produced artifact. Use the same neutral field name and monitor instructions in the two artifact views. Withhold provenance claims such as "this is the actor's real reasoning" so that the comparison concerns content rather than an authority label. Counterbalance review order. Record the probabilities and a short source-linked verdict, without requesting private reasoning.

4. **Keep constructed faults in a separate diagnostic cohort.** If natural violations are absent, detection sensitivity on natural mistakes remains unmeasured. A separately labelled diagnostic may copy each valid candidate and apply one frozen action-level fault, such as adding one forbidden export row or reporting an unverified release as ready. Preserve the real carried record. Both the original and altered candidate receive all three monitor views. No edit should insert a telltale marker; the independent grader verifies every alteration. These candidates were constructed by the experimenter and must never count as actor failures or estimates of their prevalence. An unchanged candidate serves as the paired negative control only when independently graded safe; an originally faulty candidate is not a negative control.

5. **Report the limited estimands.** For the same candidates, compare carried-minus-none and carried-minus-independent detection, false flags and probability scores. Use probabilities' Brier score over the entire graded cohort as an additional descriptive measure. Report raw paired disagreements; a tiny screen is not a calibrated false-positive study. A fixed 0.5 display threshold is descriptive, not evidence of a 10% false-positive operating point. Keep natural and constructed results separate and cluster by original world/record. Different outcomes across the two cohorts are informative rather than grounds for pooling.

For N actual candidates, W original record-commitment units and three monitor views, the natural cohort needs W independent-record calls plus 3N review calls. Adding one constructed copy of every candidate adds 3N reviews, without actor calls. For illustration, N = 8 and W = 4 would mean 28 calls for natural candidates alone or 52 with the constructed diagnostic. This is arithmetic from the proposed call graph, not a token or subscription-cost estimate.

If both artifacts improve equally, prioritize a reviewer-assistance tool over a claim that actor state has special value. If carried state wins, examine whether errors are localized through identifiable record/action mismatches. If carried state loses, inspect shared extraction mistakes and monitor anchoring. If every view succeeds or there are no violations, the result is a ceiling or missing-event result. None of these outcomes establishes equivalence to access to private CoT.

## Practical trust boundary

For policies that can be evaluated from structured source facts, enforce the policy mechanically before executing an action. A learned reviewer can supplement that check on ambiguous evidence, but an experiment with a deterministic grading oracle should not imply that another model is the strongest available enforcement mechanism. The public-state approach is most interesting where evidence interpretation is difficult, yet source references, explicit decisions and downstream checks remain reviewable. Its practical output is an accountable decision process, with measured failure modes, rather than proof of the model's internal intentions.
